## 3. What osteopathy actually is

**H2:** What is osteopathy?

**Copy:**
Osteopathy is a hands-on, whole-body form of manual therapy focused on the musculoskeletal system: your bones, joints, muscles, ligaments and the connective tissue that links them all together. In Australia it's AHPRA-registered, and osteopaths train for five years at university (a three-year clinical science degree followed by a two-year Masters of Osteopathy).

Here's the idea that makes osteopathy click for most people. Your body isn't a set of separate parts. The tissues run continuously through it, and load travels. A stiff hip can quietly show up as a sore lower back. A restricted upper back can end up as a cranky neck. So the spot that bothers you often isn't the spot causing the problem, which is exactly why treating only the sore area tends not to hold.

An osteopath's job is to work out where the load is really coming from, treat it by hand, and then help you build the area back up so it can cope with everything you want to do. Techniques can include soft tissue work, joint articulation and mobilisation, stretching, and dry needling or manipulation where it's appropriate and you're comfortable with it.

We like to know about you before we start, so there are no surprises.

When you arrive, you'll fill in a short form, then head in with your osteopath. We'll start by getting to know you: a few questions, a look at your history, and a proper conversation about your goals and what's been going on. From there we'll use a functional movement assessment to see how your body is actually moving.

Then we'll explain what we're finding and what we recommend, in plain language, before we treat you. ## 10. FAQs

**H2:** Osteopathy FAQs

**What's the difference between an osteopath, a physio and a chiro?**
All three are AHPRA-registered and the conditions they treat often overlap. The approaches differ in emphasis: physiotherapy tends to focus on movement and exercise, osteopathy takes a broad, whole-body hands-on view, and chiropractic focuses closely on the spine and joints. Honestly, at Alpha the practitioner's approach matters far more than the label, and we work as one team to get you the right care. (More on this on our blog.)

**Do I need a referral?**
No. You can book an osteopath directly.

**Can I claim on private health?**
Yes. Osteopathy is covered if you hold extras cover that includes it, with claiming available on site. Check your level of cover with your fund first.

**Do you accept WorkCover and EPC (GP care plans)?**
Yes to both.

**Is osteopathy safe? Does it hurt?**
Osteopathy is generally low-risk when provided by a registered practitioner. Some techniques feel firmer than others, and your osteo will talk you through anything before doing it, and check in as they go.

**Do you "crack" backs?**
Only if you want us to. Joint manipulation is one option among many, it's always your choice, and plenty of effective treatment happens without it. If you'd rather avoid it, just say so.

**How many appointments will I need?**
It depends on the nature and history of the issue and how you respond. We'll review your progress as we go and adjust the plan with you, rather than lock you into a set number up front.

**Can I see an osteopath while pregnant?**
Yes. Osteopathy is a common, gentle option through pregnancy and afterwards. See our pregnancy-related pain page for more.
