## Purpose

Capture the second-highest-volume service term on the site and convert readers who came for relief into patients of a clinic that will also tell them why it keeps happening.

## Reader

Someone in muscular discomfort who wants hands-on relief, framed in their own head as "I need a really good massage but a proper clinical one". Typically: the desk worker with a permanently tight neck and upper back, the lifter with a knotted upper trap, the runner with calves and glutes that never let go, the person who books a monthly massage and finds the relief lasts four days.

Crucially, this reader is often **not** at the point of committing to a treatment plan. They want to feel better this week. That is a legitimate need and the page should serve it warmly rather than immediately upselling — but it should also plant the root-cause question.

A significant sub-group is researching the term itself: myotherapy is poorly understood and frequently confused with remedial massage.

Five-second need: this is clinically trained hands-on treatment, they'll actually assess me, and I can book it now.

## Search Intent

Commercial with a strong informational shoulder. At 480/mo, `myotherapy melbourne` is the second-largest service term after `osteopath melbourne` (590) — and the keyword map flags that this service was missing from the client profile entirely, i.e. it is **under-served relative to its demand**. It deserves the full 1,400 words.

The informational queries — "what is myotherapy", "myotherapy vs remedial massage" — must be answered on-page because a large share of searchers genuinely don't know what they're buying.

### 3. What myotherapy actually is
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Genuinely explanatory, because the term is opaque. Define the scope, the training, and the assessment component that distinguishes it. This is a strong AI-extraction target.
**Key points:**
- Assessment and treatment of musculoskeletal pain and dysfunction originating in muscle and soft tissue
- Training: advanced diploma or bachelor of health science in clinical myotherapy. Myotherapists are **not** AHPRA-registered — they are self-regulated through professional associations. Say this straightforwardly; hiding it is worse than stating it.
- What a treatment includes: soft tissue and deep tissue techniques, trigger point therapy, myofascial release, dry needling, cupping, joint mobilisation, stretching, taping, and home advice
- The distinguishing feature is the assessment — testing, range of motion, and a working explanation for why the tissue is behaving that way
- 250–350 words

### 4. Myotherapy vs remedial massage
**Component:** comparison block
**Direction:** **The page's most important question and its best snippet opportunity.** Answer it fairly. Remedial massage is a good service with a different scope; the difference is assessment depth, technique range and clinical framing — not quality or effort.
**Key points:**
- **Remedial massage:** hands-on soft tissue treatment, typically diploma-level training, focused on treating the tissue presented
- **Myotherapy:** longer training, broader technique set including dry needling, and an assessment step that identifies the source of the problem rather than only its site
- Practical translation for the reader: if you want a great treatment for a tight back, either will help. If you want someone to work out why the same back keeps tightening, myotherapy is set up for that.
- Never disparage remedial massage therapists.
- Structure with a short intro, a clear two-part comparison and a one-line takeaway

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- **Myotherapists are not AHPRA-registered.** Do not describe them as registered practitioners or imply AHPRA oversight. State the actual qualification and association membership. Getting this wrong on a site that leans heavily on registration as a trust signal would be a serious inconsistency.
- Note that AHPRA advertising rules still bind the *clinic* where it employs registered practitioners, and the site as a whole should be written to the same standard throughout.
- No claims that myotherapy releases toxins, breaks down scar tissue, corrects posture or realigns anything.
- Rebate statements must be conditional — extras coverage for myotherapy varies significantly by fund and depends on the practitioner's association.
- Dry needling description must be accurate about sensation and risk. Do not undersell post-treatment soreness.
- No comparative superiority over remedial massage.
