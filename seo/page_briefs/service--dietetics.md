## Purpose

Establish dietetics as part of the recovery and training picture rather than a weight-loss service, and convert the active person whose nutrition is quietly undermining everything else they're doing.

## Reader

Three groups, and the framing must serve all three without letting the third dominate the page.

(1) The **athlete or active person** — a runner who bonks at 30km, a lifter who can't add muscle, someone whose recovery between sessions has gone backwards. They suspect fuelling is the missing piece.
(2) The **injured patient** — already at Alpha, doing everything right in rehab, and told that nutrition affects tissue healing and bone health. Often a stress-fracture or tendon patient.
(3) The **health-condition** referral — GP-referred for gut symptoms, iron deficiency, cholesterol, blood glucose or a chronic condition.

Five-second need: this is an Accredited Practising Dietitian, this is about fuelling and health rather than dieting, and rebates may apply.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- Dietitians are **not** AHPRA-registered. The credential is Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) via Dietitians Australia. Do not describe them as AHPRA-registered.
- **Weight and body composition content is the highest-risk element on this page.** No before/after imagery, no weight-loss claims, no promised kilograms, no transformation framing. If weight management is offered, describe it as health-focused and individualised, and keep it a minor element of the page rather than a headline.
- Be alert to eating disorder risk in an athletic audience. RED-S and low energy availability content should be framed supportively, and the page should not use language that could reinforce restrictive behaviour. Consider a line on when to seek specialised support.
- No claims that nutrition cures or treats disease. Frame as supporting management alongside medical care.
- Medicare CDM statements must specify GP referral, limited sessions and likely gap.
- "Dietitian vs nutritionist" must be factual and non-disparaging.

## Voice Notes

- Practical, non-judgemental, completely free of diet culture. No "clean eating", no "guilt-free", no moralising about food.
- The audience is athletic and time-poor. Talk about what to eat before a session, not about macronutrient ratios.
- Warm. This reader may have had bad experiences with nutrition advice before.
- No "performance" — which is doubly awkward here because "sports performance nutrition" is the industry term. Use "fuelling", "energy", "recovery", "getting the most out of your training".
