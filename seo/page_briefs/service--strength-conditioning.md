## Purpose

Close the loop on Alpha's integration story by showing that treatment doesn't end when the pain does — and convert existing patients into the strength phase rather than losing them at discharge.

## Reader

Almost entirely **internal traffic**. This reader is already an Alpha patient, or already on the site, reading a service or condition page and following the "what happens after rehab" thread. Very few people search their way here cold.

They are usually near the end of a rehab plan and facing the moment every clinic patient faces: the pain has settled, the exercises are getting easy, and they're about to be discharged back into the same training that broke them. Some are anxious about that. Some are impatient to get back and worried about doing it too fast.

Five-second need: there is a next step, it's structured, and it's the same team.

## Search Intent

Commercial but negligible volume — `strength and conditioning coach melbourne` is 30/mo at competition 0.20. **This is a conversion and differentiation page, not a discovery page** (keyword_map explicitly says so). Its SEO value comes from internal links, topical completeness of the services cluster, and brand. Do not over-optimise it or inflate the word count chasing volume that doesn't exist.

Its real job is to make the integration claim on the homepage and every service page *provable*. Without this page, "we stay with you through the strength phase" is an assertion. With it, it's a service.

### 2. The gap between "better" and "ready"
**Component:** `problem-statement`
**Direction:** The argument. Pain settling and being able to handle your sport are two different milestones, and the space between them is where almost all re-injury happens. Make this concrete rather than conceptual.
**Key points:**
- Worked example: the calf that stopped hurting in week four, but the runner went back to 60km weeks in week five and tore it again in week seven
- The point isn't more rest — it's a structured build back to the load your sport actually demands
- Explicitly connects to the playbook's "why your injury keeps coming back" content pillar

### 3. What we actually do
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** Concrete programme content. This reader trains and will judge the page on whether it sounds like real coaching or clinic-flavoured personal training.
**Key points:**
- Testing to establish where you are: strength, single-leg capacity, range, sport-specific movement, running metrics where relevant
- Programme design around your sport's demands, your training week and your calendar — race date, season start, event
- Progressive loading with defined criteria for each stage, so you know what "ready" means before you get there
- Technique coaching on the lifts and movements that matter for your sport
- Re-testing at set points; you see the numbers
- Delivered in our rehab space or programmed for your own gym

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- S&C delivered by a non-registered coach is not a regulated health service. Be careful not to present it as clinical treatment, and do not let it borrow the AHPRA credibility of the other services. If it is delivered by a registered practitioner, that can be stated — but the service itself should be described as coaching.
- No injury-prevention guarantees. "Reduce the risk of re-injury" is safer than "prevent re-injury", and even that should be softened to describing what the programme is designed to do.
- Do not imply health fund rebates are available if they are not.
- No disparagement of personal trainers or gyms — many are referral partners (see /about/community/).

## Voice Notes

- The most peer-to-peer, coach-to-athlete page on the site. Confident, direct, practical.
- Real training language: blocks, sets, load, testing, in-season, race week.

- Not clinical in register. This is the end of the journey, and it should feel like a step forward rather than more treatment.
