## Purpose

Hold a low-volume but high-intent term, catch the substantial long-tail around lateral hip pain and hip-versus-back confusion, and act as the sibling anchor for the back pain page.

## Reader

Three populations, and the diagnostic confusion between them is the page's main content opportunity.

1. **Lateral hip pain, mostly women 40–60.** Pain on the outside of the hip, can't lie on that side at night, sore going up stairs and standing on one leg. Usually told it's bursitis. This is the most common hip presentation in clinic and the most poorly served online.
2. **Groin and front-of-hip pain.** Younger, active, often a footballer, dancer or gym-goer. Pinching at depth in a squat, aching after sport.
3. **Deep hip / buttock pain that might be the back.** Genuinely unsure whether it's their hip, their glute or their back — and has often been treated for the wrong one.

Five-second need: *someone will actually work out which structure this is.*

### 3. Where is your hip pain?
**Component:** `condition-cards` or regional content block
**Direction:** The structural backbone, same logic as the knee page. Three regions, 100–150 words each, each written to catch its own long-tail queries.
**Key points:**
- **Outside of the hip** — gluteal tendinopathy (still widely called bursitis). Point tender over the bony bit, worse lying on that side, worse standing on one leg, worse on stairs. **Make the key correction here:** it's usually a tendon load problem rather than an inflamed bursa, which is why rest and cortisone alone often don't hold. Link → /conditions/sports-injuries/tendonitis-tendinopathy/
- **Front and groin** — hip joint related, including impingement-type pain and labral irritation. Pinching at depth, aching after activity, sometimes clicking. Note that hip osteoarthritis presents here too, typically with morning stiffness and reduced rotation.
- **Back of the hip and buttock** — often referred from the lower back or the sacroiliac joint, sometimes gluteal or hamstring-origin tendon pain, sometimes nerve-related. **This is the sibling cross-link (back ↔ hip)** → /conditions/back-pain/

### 4. Hip or back?
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Short, distinctive and genuinely useful. Almost nobody writes this and it's a common reason people get treated for months without progress.
**Key points:**
- True hip joint pain is usually felt in the groin and often deep, and typically restricts rotation
- Back-referred pain more often sits in the buttock and can travel down the leg
- Both can co-exist, and both can be aggravated by the same activities, which is why it's genuinely hard to self-diagnose
- The practical point: an examination sorts this out quickly, and getting it wrong is the most common reason hip treatment doesn't work

### 5. Lying on your side at night
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Short, practical, high-value for reader 1. Same logic as the shoulder page's night-pain block.
**Key points:**
- Why lateral hip pain is worse in bed — direct compression plus the top leg dropping across the body compresses the tendon
- Practical positioning: pillow between the knees, sleep on the less painful side, avoid crossing the top leg over
- Daytime equivalents worth naming: standing with weight shifted onto one hip, sitting cross-legged, deep low chairs
- These are load modifications, not a fix — say so

### 6. How we treat hip pain
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** The Alpha process, applied.
**Key points:**
- Work out which structure it is first — the assessment is the value
- Assess the hip, the lower back and the way you load through one leg
- Hands-on treatment to settle symptoms
- Progressive strengthening, particularly the glutes, loaded properly — and note honestly that for gluteal tendinopathy the standard stretching and foam rolling advice often aggravates it, because it compresses the tendon
- Load management: sitting, standing, sleeping positions, and running or training volume
- Timeframes: tendon-related hip pain typically responds over months, not weeks

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- Bursitis reframe must be measured — "often" and "usually", not "always". Bursitis does exist.
- No claim that treatment resolves gluteal tendinopathy. Describe the approach and the honest timeline.
- Cortisone handled neutrally — describe the trade-off, defer to the treating doctor.
- Hip osteoarthritis and hip replacement decisions belong with the patient and their surgeon. Do not steer.
- The stretching caution must be framed as "for this presentation, stretching can aggravate", not as blanket advice against stretching.
- No claim to diagnose from the page. Section 4 helps someone understand the distinction; it does not let them make it.
