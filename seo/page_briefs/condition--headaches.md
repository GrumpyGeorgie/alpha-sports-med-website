## Purpose

Take treatment intent for headaches, position Alpha honestly within a condition that has a large medical component, and convert the reader whose headaches have a neck driver that nobody has assessed.

## Reader

Has had headaches for a long time — often years. Has usually tried painkillers, may have been to a GP, may have a migraine diagnosis and medication. Has probably never had their neck examined by anyone.

Emotional state: tired, and quietly resigned. Headaches get minimised by everyone around them and often by the person themselves. Many have stopped mentioning it. The frequency has crept up and they've adapted their life around it — declining things, keeping medication in every bag.

Five-second need: *there might be a physical driver here that nobody has looked at.*

### 2. Red flags
**Component:** alert block
**Direction:** **Mandatory and above the fold.** Headache has genuinely serious differentials and this page will be read by people with new or changing symptoms.
**Key points:**
- **Call 000 or go to an emergency department for:** a sudden severe headache that peaks within seconds or minutes ("worst headache of my life"), headache with fever and neck stiffness, headache with confusion, weakness, slurred speech or visual loss, headache after a head injury, or a first severe headache with seizure
- **See your GP promptly for:** a headache pattern that has changed, a new headache after age 50, headache that's worse lying down or with coughing or straining, headache with unexplained weight loss, or headaches that are becoming more frequent
- **Also see your GP if you're using pain relief for headaches more than a couple of days a week** — medication overuse headache is common, under-recognised, and directly relevant to this reader. This is a genuinely useful inclusion.

### 4. Where headaches come from
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Educational and honest about scope. Cover the main types briefly, then narrow to what Alpha can genuinely assess and treat.
**Key points:**
- Tension-type headaches — the most common, band-like, both sides, often stress and load related
- Migraine — described accurately (one-sided, throbbing, nausea, light and sound sensitivity, sometimes aura), and explicitly noted as a neurological condition with medical management. Alpha's role here is adjunctive and must be described as such.
- **Cervicogenic headache** — the page's centre of gravity. Referred from the upper neck joints, typically one-sided, starting at the base of the skull, coming over the head or behind the eye, often with neck stiffness, provoked by sustained positions.
- Note the overlap: many people have more than one type, and neck contribution can co-exist with migraine. This is why partial results are so common.
- Jaw and clenching as a contributor → /conditions/tmj-jaw-pain/

### 5. The neck connection
**Component:** `problem-statement`
**Direction:** **The conversion argument.** Make it specific enough to be testable against the reader's own experience.
**Key points:**
- The upper cervical joints refer pain into the head — an established mechanism, described plainly
- What suggests a neck contribution: one-sidedness that stays on the same side, onset at the base of the skull, provocation by desk work or driving, associated neck stiffness, reproduction of familiar head pain when the neck is examined
- Why it gets missed: headaches get treated as a medication question, and the neck rarely gets examined
- Be measured — "may contribute", "in some people". Do not claim most headaches are cervicogenic.
- Link across: → /conditions/neck-pain/ and → /conditions/tmj-jaw-pain/

### 6. How we approach headaches
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** Show the assessment, and state the boundary clearly.
**Key points:**
- History first, and properly: pattern, timing, triggers, what you've tried, what medication you use and how often
- Examine the neck, upper back and jaw — including whether pressure on specific neck segments reproduces your familiar headache, which is the key clinical question
- **State the boundary on the page:** we don't diagnose migraine and we don't manage medication — that's your GP. If your headaches need medical review, we'll say so.
- Treatment where a musculoskeletal contribution is found: hands-on work to the upper neck and surrounding soft tissue, dry needling where indicated, restoring movement
- The longer game: neck and shoulder-blade strength, screen and desk habits, sleep, stress load, and jaw habits
- Track frequency and intensity rather than chasing single-episode relief — the honest measure of whether this is working

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

**High clinical risk. Third-highest in the batch after concussion and rib.**

- The red flags block is mandatory and must include thunderclap headache and post-trauma headache.
- **No claim that treatment cures, prevents or reduces migraines.** Migraine is a neurological condition with medical management. The permissible framing is that where a musculoskeletal contribution is present, addressing it may help, and that this sits alongside medical care.
- **Never suggest reducing or stopping medication.** The FAQ answer must explicitly defer to the prescriber.
- Do not claim to treat migraine. Treat the musculoskeletal contribution to headache. The distinction is small in wording and large in compliance.
- Medication overuse headache must be framed as "worth discussing with your GP", never as advice to stop.
- Alpha does not diagnose headache disorders — state it on the page.
- Cervicogenic mechanism claims stay measured: "can", "may", "in some people".
