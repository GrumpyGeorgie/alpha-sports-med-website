## Purpose

Be the page that says *we believe you* to people who have spent years not being believed — and be scrupulously honest about the narrow slice of that experience Alpha can actually help with.

## Reader

The most sensitive audience on the site, and the one most likely to close the tab if the tone is wrong by a single degree.

She has a condition — diagnosed or not — that does not show. Fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue / ME, POTS or dysautonomia, hypermobility or hEDS, endometriosis, long COVID, persistent pain, an autoimmune condition. She looks fine. She has been told she looks fine, often as though it settles the matter.

What she carries into this page is not primarily a clinical need. It is **years of accumulated dismissal.** Test results that came back normal and were treated as an answer rather than a question. Being told it's stress, or anxiety, or that she should try yoga. Being labelled a difficult patient for persisting. Having to perform her symptoms to be taken seriously, then being disbelieved for performing them.

She is also **extremely good at detecting insincerity**. This community shares notes. A page that overclaims, that lists conditions the clinic can't help with, or that uses "wellness journey" language will be recognised as marketing within one paragraph and she will be gone.

She has usually also learned to be careful with her energy. Booking, travelling to and recovering from an appointment is a genuine cost she has to budget for. That must be acknowledged.

**First five seconds must deliver:** we believe you, we're not going to tell you it's in your head, and here is honestly what we can and can't do.

## Search Intent

**Predominantly informational and community-seeking, not commercial.** This is the important strategic point and it should shape everything: `invisible illness` (260/mo) is largely searched by people looking for recognition, language, community and validation — not for a physiotherapist.

**Do not brief or measure this page as a booking page.** Its jobs, in order:

1. **Trust and brand.** It signals what kind of clinic Alpha is to a highly networked community that shares recommendations aggressively. Its main conversion path is word of mouth, not the CTA.
2. **Support the hypermobility page.** High audience overlap; this is the softer entry point into the site's most commercially valuable specialty.
3. **Capture a small, high-intent tail** — people with a named invisible condition looking for a practitioner who won't dismiss them.

A modest word count with an immaculate tone will outperform a longer page that tries to convert harder.

## Sections

### 1. Hero
**Component:** `hero-banner`
**Direction:** Quiet. Plain. No stock imagery of anyone looking wistfully out a window — that visual language is exactly what this audience finds patronising. Consider a typographic hero.
**Key points:**
- H1 containing "invisible illness"
- Subhead: you look fine. You are not fine. We start from believing you.
- **Do not put a booking CTA in the hero.** It is the wrong first move for this reader and it will read as transactional. Book online belongs further down, after the page has earned it.

### 2. You look fine
**Component:** `problem-statement`
**Direction:** **~250 words — the longest problem statement on the site, and the single most important section here.** This is the playbook's "not being listened to" pain point in its purest form. Write it as recognition, specific and unsentimental. If it reads as sympathy, it has failed.
**Key points:**
- The specific, recognisable experiences: normal test results treated as a verdict; "you look well!"; the exhaustion that sleep doesn't touch; the good day that costs three bad ones; cancelling on people again; having to decide whether today's outing is worth tomorrow; the mental arithmetic of energy
- The medical experience: being told it's stress. Being told to try yoga. Being asked if you're sure. Having to bring a list, and being judged for the list. Being the patient who "keeps coming back".
- **The boom-and-bust cycle** — feel better, do everything, pay for it for a week. Name it explicitly; almost nobody puts it in writing and everyone with an invisible illness lives it.
- Grief, briefly and without drama: for the version of your life you expected. Do not dwell, but do not skip it either — its absence from every other clinic page is conspicuous.
- **The rule for this section:** never criticise the practitioners who dismissed her. Describe the experience, not the people. She is not looking for someone to be angry on her behalf — she is looking for someone who recognises it and is calm about it.
- **Close on the pivot:** we can't fix all of that. Here's the part we can help with.

### 3. What we can help with — and what we can't
**Component:** two-column or clearly-delineated content block
**Direction:** **The page's integrity section and its strongest trust signal.** As on the paediatric page, restraint reads as expertise. She has been overpromised by people selling supplements, programs and cures for years, and the willingness to say "not that" is the thing that distinguishes Alpha from them.
**Key points:**
- **What we work with:** musculoskeletal pain and its management; joint instability; deconditioning and safely rebuilding strength; graded activity and pacing; movement and function; helping you work out how much you can do and how to spread it; hands-on treatment where it genuinely helps
- **What we do not do, stated plainly:** we do not diagnose fibromyalgia, ME/CFS, POTS, autoimmune conditions or endometriosis. We do not treat the underlying condition. **We do not have a cure and we're not going to imply we do.** We are not a replacement for your GP, rheumatologist, immunologist, gynaecologist or specialist.
- **Where we fit:** alongside your medical team, on the movement, strength and pain-management side
- One honest sentence about limits: some people we see get a lot out of this. Some don't. We'll tell you which way it's going rather than booking you in indefinitely.
- **Do not publish a long list of named conditions "we treat".** Name conditions only as context for the kind of experience being described — never as a treatment claim. This is the page's biggest scope trap.

### 4. What working with us actually looks like
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** Concrete and calibrated to someone with limited energy. Every step should implicitly answer "will this cost me more than it gives me".
**Key points:**
- **A first appointment with enough time.** She has a history that takes longer than ten minutes to tell and has been rushed through it before.
- **Assessment that starts with your day, not your joints** — what you can do, what it costs you, what you've had to give up
- **Pacing and graded activity**, explained properly. Not "push through". Not "rest more". The middle path, which almost nobody explains well.
- **Flare management** — what to do on a bad day and how to get back on track without losing everything. Name flares. It is her vocabulary.
- **Very gradual progression**, and honesty that progress is measured in months
- **You set the pace.** If today isn't a treatment day, we adjust. Nothing is done to you that you didn't agree to.
- **Online consults** — genuinely significant for someone for whom travel is a cost. Give this real prominence, not a footnote. Link `/services/online-consults/`.
- **Working with your existing team**, not around them
- **Multidisciplinary under one roof** — fewer separate appointments, fewer retellings of the same story. Frame the benefit as *energy saved*, which is what she actually cares about.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

**Scope of practice is the dominant risk on this page — higher than the claims risk.**

- **Do not imply Alpha treats fibromyalgia, ME/CFS, POTS, dysautonomia, endometriosis, autoimmune conditions or long COVID.** Alpha may work with people who have them, on the musculoskeletal and activity side. The distinction between *treating a condition* and *working with a person who has one* must be explicit in the copy, not just implied.
- No diagnosis of any of the above.
- No cure, remission, reversal or "root cause of your fatigue" language. **"Root cause" is a playbook phrase and it is unsafe here** — it implies Alpha can explain a condition medicine hasn't. Flag this to the writer explicitly; it is an easy accidental breach.
- No claims about immune function, inflammation, nervous system "regulation" or "resetting".
- **Testimonials are very high-risk** on this page and are best omitted entirely. A patient saying "they helped when nobody else could" is an implied superiority claim about other practitioners as well as an outcome claim.
- No comparative or implied-superiority claims about the practitioners who dismissed her, however sympathetically framed.
- Mental health content is signposting only.
- Named clinician sign-off, with specific attention to the scope boundaries in §3.

## Voice Notes

- **The most carefully calibrated page on the site.** Get the tone wrong and it does active brand damage with a community that talks to each other.
- Believe first. Explain second. Offer third. In that order, without exception.
- **Banned language:** "wellness journey", "holistic healing", "listen to your body", "self-care", "warrior", "battle", "suffering from", "despite her illness", "inspirational". Every one of these is a tell that the writer hasn't spoken to anyone with an invisible illness.
- Do not use "you're so strong" framing. She is tired of being told she's strong; she'd like to be tired less.
- Do not imply positive thinking, stress management or mindset are the missing piece.
- Say "people with", not "sufferers".
- **Understate.** Alpha's usual energy is wrong here. This is the quietest page on the site alongside paediatric osteopathy.
- No "performance". The relevant goal is a life with less cost attached to it, not a comeback.
