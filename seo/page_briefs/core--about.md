## Purpose

Turn brand curiosity into trust by telling the founders' story honestly — why Alpha exists, what it refuses to do, and what that means for the person reading.

## Reader

Someone who has already decided Alpha *might* be the answer and is now checking whether these people are for real. Often they arrived from the homepage or a condition page, mid-consideration, deciding between Alpha and the cheaper clinic down the road. A meaningful minority are prospective practitioners checking the culture before applying.

They need, in five seconds: this is a clinic with a point of view, founded by people who were themselves frustrated with how healthcare treated them, and it has been running long enough to be trusted.

## Search Intent

Navigational and evaluative. Nobody types this to learn about osteopathy. They type it to decide whether to trust a business. That means the page's job is character, not credentials — credentials live on /about/qualifications/.

## Sections

### 1. Hero
**Component:** `hero-banner`
**Direction:** Open with the founding tension, not a mission statement. Alpha exists because two osteopaths couldn't find a clinic they'd want to be treated in.
**Key points:**
- H1 with brand
- One-line positioning: the place people come when nothing else has worked
- Since 2017 · three clinics · Melbourne's west

### 2. Why Alpha exists
**Component:** `problem-statement`
**Direction:** This is the story section and the best copy on the page. Use Ashton's own words — the jellyfish keeper line is disarming, memorable and completely on-brand for "fun, authentic, takes the work seriously not themselves". It also earns the reader's trust because it starts with failure, not success.
**Key points:**
- Quote (verify permission to publish): *"I was so disheartened by my first experience as an osteo I applied to be a jellyfish keeper at SeaWorld. I felt so lost within this profession, like I didn't fit the mould. So I decided to make my own."*
- 2017: Ashton saw the gap in Melbourne's west between traditional allied health and exercise-based rehab. He didn't want to fill the gap — he wanted to build the clinic he could never find.
- Will's arc: aged care, graduate year at Alpha, left to build senior/leadership experience, came back to co-found. Quote: *"3 years into my work as just an Osteopath, I wanted more."*
- Started at Maidstone, which became Ascot Vale. Three clinics now.
- The two patient promises side by side: Ashton wants patients to feel *seen and heard*; Will wants them to feel *empowered*. Validation and empowerment. Name this as the two halves of the Alpha experience.

### 3. What we were frustrated by
**Component:** `problem-statement` (secondary) or a simple list block
**Direction:** State the disruptive spirit plainly. This is where the reader who has been burned recognises their own experience. Keep it about systems and defaults, never about individual practitioners.
**Key points:**
- Textbook treatment that isn't patient-centred
- "Stop running, stop gym, rest" as the default answer
- One-sided, modality-first thinking
- Clinics that don't actually listen
- Turn: Alpha is the answer to all four. Do not name a competitor. Do not use the word "better".

### 4. How that shows up in your appointment
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** Convert philosophy into observable behaviour, otherwise this page is just vibes. Each value should have a concrete practice attached.
**Key points:**
- Genuine care → the initial consult is 45 minutes because listening takes time
- Education → you leave understanding more about your body than when you came in; the plan is explained before you're asked to commit to it
- Integration → hands-on treatment and exercise rehab are one plan, not two referrals; the person who treats you talks to the person who programmes for you
- Community → we know our patients and their clubs, gyms and races
- Authenticity → activewear, not scrubs; no scripts; "just like you, but confident in what we know"
- End every session with a re-test

### 5. What we won't do
**Component:** `problem-statement` variant
**Direction:** A short, confident block. This is the most differentiating copy available and almost no clinic writes it. It also pre-qualifies the reader, which matches the "we don't chase" sales philosophy.
**Key points:**
- We won't promise a quick fix
- We won't discharge you the moment the pain settles
- We won't tell you to just rest and hope
- We won't compete on price — and we'll happily explain why our fees are what they are
- Handle the last one carefully: confident, not defensive, and never dismissive of cost-conscious patients

### 6. Where we're heading
**Component:** standard content block
**Direction:** The 10-year vision, briefly. It signals a business that is building something, which reassures a patient starting a long treatment journey.
**Key points:**
- Will's framing is the usable one: Alpha as a feeling, not just a place — community, leaders, legacy
- Keep to 80–100 words; this is not an investor deck

### 7. The team
**Component:** `practitioner-cards` (preview)
**Direction:** 4–6 faces, then push to /about/team/ and /about/qualifications/.

### 8. CTA
**Component:** `cta-section`
**Direction:** Low pressure, in keeping with "we shine brightly and patients who are ready come to us". Offer both booking and the tell-us-what's-going-on path.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- Founder quotes are opinions about their own careers, not clinical claims — safe.
- "The place people go when nothing else has worked" is positioning, not an outcome promise. Keep it as a description of who comes, not what happens to them.
- Avoid any implication of superiority over other clinics when writing section 3. Frame as what frustrated the founders about industry defaults, not about named or implied competitors.
- No before/after imagery.

## Voice Notes

- The most personal page on the site. First person plural throughout, with named first-person quotes from the founders.
- Warmth and humour are on-brand here in a way they aren't on a condition page. The SeaWorld line should land as a laugh, then a point.
- No "performance". Ashton's own vision statement in the playbook uses "performance-led living" — rewrite that as "getting back to doing what you love" if you draw on it.
