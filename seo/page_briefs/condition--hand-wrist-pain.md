## Purpose

Hold a surprisingly large head term (`hand pain` 1,000 + `wrist pain treatment` 480) at very low competition, and serve three distinct populations — the climber, the new parent, and the desk worker with nerve symptoms.

## Reader

1. **The gripper.** Climbers, boulderers, gym-goers, and tradies. Finger, thumb and wrist pain from grip load. Highly motivated, highly informed, will not stop training. Alpha already has a live post on climbing hand pain, which says something about this population's presence in the patient base.
2. **The new parent.** Thumb and wrist pain from lifting a baby — classic de Quervain's. Extremely common, rarely written about, and this reader is exhausted and can't stop the aggravating activity, which is the whole clinical problem.
3. **The desk worker with nerve symptoms.** Numbness and tingling in the hand, worse at night, shaking the hand out to relieve it. Suspects carpal tunnel and is worried about surgery.

Common thread: the hand is impossible to rest. Every treatment plan has to work around that, and saying so is the page's fastest credibility win.

## Search Intent

Commercial with an informational lean. `hand pain` (1,000, comp 0.02) is unusually uncompetitive for its volume — the term is broad and most clinics don't target it. Winnable.

The long-tail is where the specific intent lives: carpal tunnel, de Quervain's, thumb base pain, trigger finger, wrist pain with pressing. Section structure below is built around those.

**Scope honesty:** some hand conditions sit with hand therapists, rheumatologists or surgeons. The page should be clear about what Alpha treats and refer confidently for what it doesn't. That candour is more persuasive than claiming everything.

### 4. Why hands are hard to settle
**Component:** `problem-statement`
**Direction:** The argument, and the differentiator.
**Key points:**
- You can rest a knee. You cannot rest the hands that hold a baby, a keyboard, a drill or a steering wheel.
- Which is why "rest it for six weeks" is advice most people simply can't follow, and then feel like they failed at
- The alternative: work out which specific movements load the painful structure and change those, rather than trying to stop everything
- Concrete examples — how you lift the baby, grip a mouse, hold a bar, set up a keyboard, use a screwdriver, hold a phone
- Bracing and taping as genuinely useful short-term tools for the hand, more so than most body parts, framed as support during loading rather than treatment

### 5. How we treat it
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** Practical, with an explicit scope boundary.
**Key points:**
- Work out which structure and which movements — the hand rewards a precise assessment more than almost anywhere
- Assess the wrist, elbow, shoulder and neck, because nerve symptoms in the hand often start higher up
- Hands-on treatment to the wrist, forearm and hand
- Grip and forearm strengthening, progressed — the part that builds tolerance
- Task and equipment modification: mouse and keyboard, lifting technique, tool grips, how the baby gets picked up
- **State the boundary:** we'll refer to a hand therapist, GP or specialist where that's the right call — fractures, suspected rheumatoid or inflammatory arthritis, significant nerve compression, or acute tendon and pulley injuries

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- **Nerve symptoms need a clear escalation line.** Persistent numbness, weakness, or visible muscle wasting at the base of the thumb requires medical review — state it. Getting this wrong risks a delayed diagnosis.
- Wrist pain after a fall: state that imaging may be needed to exclude a scaphoid fracture, which is commonly missed and has real consequences. This is a genuine patient-safety inclusion.
- Do not claim to treat inflammatory arthritis. Refer.
- No claim that treatment avoids carpal tunnel surgery. Neutral, defers to the doctor.
- Scope boundary in section 5 must be genuine, not decorative — it should reflect what Alpha actually refers on.
- No claim to diagnose.
