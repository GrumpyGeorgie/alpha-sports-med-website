## Purpose

Convert the frustrated, already-tried-everything active person into a booking by showing them Alpha understands their situation before naming a single modality.

## Reader

Two arrivals. The larger group is brand-aware: they were told about Alpha by a gym mate, a run club member, or their partner, and they are checking Alpha is legitimate before they book. The smaller group landed on a broad commercial term ("sports injury clinic", "sports medicine clinic melbourne") and is comparison shopping.

Both are in the same emotional state described in the playbook — lost, frustrated, a bit scared, resigned, and over-Googled. They have seen someone before. They got a sheet of exercises or a rub-down and it came back. They are quietly wondering whether this is just their life now.

In the first five seconds they need: *these people deal with exactly my problem, they are not going to tell me to rest, and I can book right now without a referral.*

## Search Intent

Mostly navigational — verify the brand and find the booking button. The commercial slice wants to know whether this clinic is a real multidisciplinary operation or a single-practitioner room with a sports-sounding name. Neither group is searching a modality. **Do not build the homepage around the service list.**

## Sections

### 1. Hero
**Component:** `hero-banner`
**Direction:** Lead with the *situation*, not the service menu. The strategic job of this page is shifting "problem → modality" into "problem → Alpha", and that starts in the H1. Write the headline about a person who has already tried things and is still stuck. Sub-headline earns the click by naming what is different: hands-on treatment and exercise rehab run as one plan, not two appointments.
**Key points:**

- Angle to test: "The pain keeps coming back. Let's find out why." / "You've tried things. They haven't stuck."
- Sub-head: multidisciplinary team, three clinics across Melbourne's west, treatment and rehab under one roof
- Primary CTA: Book online (Cliniko). Secondary: (03) 9962 5539

### 2. Problem statement — "Sound familiar?"
**Component:** `problem-statement`
**Direction:** Validate before you educate. This is the highest-value block on the page and the one most homepages skip. Mirror the reader's exact internal monologue back at them in short, plain sentences, then land the turn: the reason it keeps coming back is that nobody has found the root cause yet.
**Key points:**
- Use the playbook's five concerns almost verbatim as the source material: nothing has worked; scared of making it worse; wasn't listened to; is this another expensive thing that fails; do I even need a physio or an osteo?
- Explicitly defuse the "stop running, stop gym, rest" advice — Alpha's position is that rest has its place but is rarely the whole answer, and a load-managed return beats complete rest
- Close on the North Star: we get to the root of the problem
- Never trash-talk the previous practitioner. Frame it as "textbook treatment, not tailored treatment"

### 3. Don't know who to book with? Start with the problem
**Component:** `condition-cards`
**Direction:** This is the section that operationalises the positioning shift. Instead of asking the reader to self-diagnose which discipline they need, ask what hurts and what it is stopping them doing. Cards route to condition pages, not service pages.
**Key points:**
- 8–9 cards max: Knee Pain, Back Pain, Shoulder Pain, Neck Pain & Headaches, Foot Pain & Plantar Fasciitis, Running Injuries, Sports Injuries, Pregnancy-Related Pain, Hypermobility & EDS
- Micro-line above the grid: "You don't need to know whether you need a physio, an osteo or a chiro. That's our job."
- This directly answers pain point 5 (modality confusion) and is the on-page proof of the "we treat the problem, not the modality" differentiator

### 4. How we work — the Alpha approach
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** Show the *how*, because per the playbook people don't choose Alpha first simply because they never see the difference early enough. Three or four steps, each concrete enough that the reader can picture the appointment.
**Key points:**
- Step 1 — We listen first. The initial consultation is about understanding the person, not just the injury. Name the questions actually asked: what have you already tried, what is this stopping you doing, what do you want to get back to.
- Step 2 — We find the root cause. Assessment looks past the painful bit to the thing loading it.
- Step 3 — Hands-on treatment *and* a plan. Not a photocopied exercise sheet — a programme built around your training, your week and your goal.
- Step 4 — We stay with you through the strength phase. Most clinics treat then discharge. Say this without naming or knocking anyone.
- Mention the re-test at the end of every session — patients leave feeling better than when they arrived. This is a genuine, checkable process claim, not an outcome claim.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- No outcome guarantees anywhere. "We find out why" is a process claim and is safe; "we fix your pain" is not.
- The re-test claim ("you leave feeling better than you arrived") is a description of clinical process and internal standard — keep it framed that way, not as a promised therapeutic result.
- Any testimonial or star-rating block must not imply guaranteed results. Prefer quotes about *understanding and having a plan* over quotes about being cured. Written consent required.
- No before/after imagery.
- No comparative superiority. "Most clinics treat then discharge" is borderline — write it as what Alpha does ("we stay with you through the strength phase") rather than what others fail to do.

## Voice Notes

- This page carries more emotional weight than any other on the site. Validate in section 2 before you educate anywhere else.
- Casual but authoritative. Short sentences in the problem block, longer in the approach block.
- Banned: "performance", "quick fix", "instant results", "guaranteed".
- Use: root cause, tailored, get back to, we get it, we have your back, multidisciplinary, integrative, community.
