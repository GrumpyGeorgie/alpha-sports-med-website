## Purpose

Capture `chiropractor ascot vale` (90/mo) and — more valuably — intercept the reader who has typed "chiropractor" as a guess, and give them a path into Alpha's multidisciplinary model without making them feel wrong for guessing.

## Reader

Someone in Ascot Vale, Moonee Ponds or Maribyrnong with back or neck pain who has typed "chiropractor" for one of three reasons: they've seen one before and it helped, someone told them to, or it is simply the word they associate with a bad back. The third group is the largest and the most interesting.

The playbook names this precisely as pain point 5: **"Do I need a physio? An osteo? A chiro? What's the difference?"** Most of this page's readers do not have a confident answer. The clinic's own objection-handling script says the modality matters far less than the practitioner's approach.

The trap this page must avoid: telling the reader their choice was wrong. That is condescending, it violates the playbook's rule about never talking down to patients about their choices, and it loses the booking. The correct move is to answer the question they asked, properly, and let the breadth become apparent.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

The highest-risk page in the batch for two reasons. First, **chiropractic advertising is under specific and ongoing AHPRA scrutiny** — claims about spinal adjustment producing effects beyond musculoskeletal symptoms (immunity, general wellness, paediatric conditions, "subluxation" framing) are actively enforced against. Keep the entire page inside musculoskeletal assessment and treatment. No wellness or maintenance-care claims. No spinal-health-equals-general-health framing.

Second, **the modality comparison section in section 5 is a comparative-claims minefield.** The playbook rule and the AHPRA rule align: never suggest one modality is better. The safe construction is that the approaches overlap substantially and the practitioner and plan matter more — which is Alpha's genuine clinical position, not a hedge. Have this section reviewed before publication.

Also: do not use "chiropractor" and "Dr" in a way that could imply medical qualification without the discipline being clear. Australian convention requires the discipline be evident where the title is used.
