## Purpose

Capture the ACL rehab searcher — almost always either waiting for surgery or recovering from it — and position Alpha honestly as the rehab provider, not as an alternative to a surgical decision.

## Reader

Two clearly separated states, and the page must serve them in sequence rather than blending them.

1. **Pre-surgical / newly injured.** Knee gave way, heard or felt a pop, swelled within hours. Possibly has an MRI result, possibly waiting for one. Frightened, and Googling "can you live without an ACL" and "do I definitely need surgery". Emotionally raw — for many this is the first serious injury of their life and the immediate thought is about the season, the sport, the year.
2. **Post-surgical.** Has had a reconstruction, has a surgeon's protocol, and is looking for somewhere to do the twelve months of rehab. Comparison shopping on competence and equipment. Much more practical, much less frightened.

Five-second need: *these people do ACL rehab properly and work with my surgeon.*

## Search Intent

Commercial. `acl injury treatment` (1,300) and `acl rupture treatment` (720) are service queries; the far larger `acl tear` (4,400) is informational and is not this page's target. Competition is low (≤0.09) across the cluster.

Critical intent nuance: a meaningful share of these searchers are trying to find out whether they can avoid surgery. **The page must not monetise that hope.** It answers the question honestly, which — done well — is more persuasive than the alternative.

### 3. What's actually happened
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Short, clear anatomy and mechanism. **Salvage `/education_hub/acl-rupture/` and the live `/acl-rupture/` page here.** Keep it functional — this reader wants orientation, not a lecture.
**Key points:**
- What the ACL does (rotational and forward stability of the knee) in one plain sentence
- Typical mechanism: change of direction, deceleration, landing — usually non-contact, which surprises people
- Common signs: the pop, rapid swelling, a knee that felt unstable
- Associated injuries are common (meniscus, MCL, cartilage) — mention so an MRI report doesn't blindside them
- Say plainly that diagnosis is confirmed by clinical assessment plus MRI, and that we will refer if that hasn't happened

### 4. Surgery or not — the honest version
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** **The page's integrity test and its most important section.** Handle with care and without a commercial agenda. Get the framing right and this becomes the section people screenshot and send to their partner.
**Key points:**
- The decision is made between the patient and an orthopaedic surgeon, taking into account age, sport, associated injuries, knee stability and goals. It is not a decision Alpha makes.
- **Never imply surgery can be avoided.** Do not say "many people avoid surgery with rehab". State neutrally that non-surgical management is an established pathway for some people in some circumstances, that it is a genuine clinical discussion, and that the evidence is still evolving.
- Be explicit that a torn ACL does not heal back to its previous state on its own, and that rehab does not reconstruct a ligament.
- What rehab does do in the non-surgical pathway: build the strength and control that lets the rest of the knee compensate. Framed as management, not repair.
- Clear signpost: if you haven't seen a surgeon and you're deciding, we'll help you get that appointment. That's the referral, not the sales pitch.

### 5. Prehab: the weeks before surgery
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** The commercially valuable section that almost no competitor page writes well, and the one that captures reader type 1 in a way that doesn't require any overclaiming.
**Key points:**
- Why the state of the knee going into surgery matters — the argument for not just sitting and waiting
- The realistic goals: settle swelling, restore full extension, wake the quads up, get walking normally
- Frame as commonly recommended practice, associated with a smoother early recovery. Do not state it as a guaranteed determinant of outcome.
- Typical window: however many weeks the surgical list gives you, and we build around that date

### 6. After surgery
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** Reader type 2's section. Practical, staged, and explicit that the surgeon's protocol leads.
**Key points:**
- We follow your surgeon's protocol and communicate with them — say this plainly, it is the primary trust signal on the page
- Broad phases described as a progression rather than a calendar: early protection and range, strength rebuilding, running and landing, change of direction, sport-specific
- Emphasise that the timeline is criteria-driven and long — roughly nine to twelve months to sport is the standard framing, and being straight about that builds more trust than optimism
- What's on site: exercise physiology, clinical pilates, S&C — the point being you don't get discharged at the gym-referral stage
- **Return-to-sport framing:** we assess strength, hop and control benchmarks and share those results with you and your surgeon. **Alpha does not clear anyone to return to sport — that decision sits with the treating surgeon and medical team.** State this on the page, not just in this brief.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

**Highest-integrity-risk page after concussion.**

- **Never imply surgery is avoidable.** Not in a heading, not in a testimonial, not in a meta description. The commercial temptation here is real and the answer is no.
- Do not state or imply that Alpha determines the surgical pathway.
- No outcome claims about graft integrity, re-rupture rates, or return-to-sport success. If any statistic is used it must be attributed and framed as population-level, not personal.
- Return-to-sport clearance must be attributed to the surgeon/medical team. Alpha provides assessment data; it does not clear.
- Prehab benefits framed as "commonly recommended" and "associated with", never "ensures" or "leads to".
- No before/after imagery.
