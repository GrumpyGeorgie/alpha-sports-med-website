## Purpose

Answer "are these people actually qualified?" with specifics, and convert the reader's uncertainty about *which* discipline is legitimate into confidence in the team as a whole.

## Reader

The sceptic and the researcher. Often someone who has been told by a GP, a friend or the internet that one of these professions is less rigorous than another — the "is osteopathy even real?" or "chiropractors aren't proper doctors, are they?" reader. Also: parents booking paediatric osteopathy, patients with complex conditions who have been let down by underqualified care, and referrers doing due diligence.

They need, fast: every practitioner here is university-qualified and AHPRA-registered, and here is exactly what that training involves.

## Search Intent

Informational with high trust weight. **No primary keyword** — the page's job is E-E-A-T, not demand capture, and a brand-prefix term ("alpha sports medicine qualifications") would be a fabricated target with no volume. The genuine long-tail terms retained as secondaries ("are osteopaths qualified", "ahpra registered osteopath melbourne") are the only search targets here. This page is a citation target for AI answer engines asking "is Alpha Sports Medicine legitimate" and a supporting authority signal for every service page that links to it. Write it to be quotable: clear, factual, structured, no marketing froth.

## Sections

### 1. Hero
**Component:** `hero-banner`
**Direction:** Plain and factual. No hype. State the top-line: every clinician at Alpha holds a university qualification in their discipline and is registered with AHPRA (or, for exercise physiologists and dietitians, with their national accrediting body).
**Key points:**
- H1
- Note the two regulatory paths up front, because this is the detail most clinic pages get wrong: osteopaths, physiotherapists and chiropractors are AHPRA-registered; exercise physiologists are ESSA-accredited (AEP); dietitians are Accredited Practising Dietitians via Dietitians Australia; myotherapists are self-regulated with membership bodies. Being straight about this builds more trust than pretending everyone is registered the same way.

### 2. What each discipline actually studies
**Component:** `service-cards` variant / accordion
**Direction:** One block per discipline: the degree, the length, the registration body, and — critically — one line on what that training is *for*. This is the section that quietly answers "what's the difference between an osteo and a physio?" without turning into a comparison page (that content stays on the blog).
**Key points:**
- **Osteopathy** — five years of university study (typically a bachelor's plus a master's), AHPRA-registered via the Osteopathy Board of Australia, annual CPD requirements
- **Physiotherapy** — four-year bachelor or graduate-entry master's, AHPRA-registered via the Physiotherapy Board of Australia
- **Chiropractic** — five years of university study, AHPRA-registered via the Chiropractic Board of Australia
- **Exercise Physiology** — four-year degree, ESSA-accredited Exercise Physiologist (AEP), Medicare-eligible under a GP Chronic Disease Management plan
- **Dietetics** — accredited degree plus Accredited Practising Dietitian (APD) credential
- **Myotherapy** — advanced diploma or bachelor of health science (clinical myotherapy)
- **Clinical Pilates / S&C** — delivered by the above clinicians with additional post-graduate certification, not by fitness instructors. Say this plainly; it is the differentiator that matters most on those two service pages.
- Verify every number and body name before publication. Getting a degree length wrong on the credibility page is a self-inflicted wound.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- **Highest-risk page on the site for title and credential misuse.** Every use of "Dr" must be immediately qualified with the profession.
- Do not use "specialist" or "specialises in" unless the practitioner holds recognised specialist registration. Use "special interest in" or "additional training in".
- No comparative claims about which discipline is better trained. The whole point of this page is that they are all legitimate.
- Medicare and health fund claiming statements must be accurate and conservative — incorrect rebate claims are both an AHPRA and an ACCC problem. State eligibility conditions, not just entitlements.
- Do not imply that qualifications guarantee outcomes.
