## Purpose

Take treatment intent under a 6,600/mo head term, and connect neck pain to the two things it most often drives — headaches and jaw pain — so the reader recognises the pattern they've been treating in pieces.

## Reader

Predominantly desk-based, mid-30s to 50s, with a neck that has been "a bit stiff" for years and has now become a problem. Often reports it's worse by Thursday, worse in busy periods, worse after long drives or a bad night's sleep.

A significant proportion also get headaches and haven't connected the two. Some have jaw pain or clench at night and haven't connected that either. Making those connections on the page is the highest-value thing it can do.

Secondary arrivals: acute wry neck (woke up unable to turn their head — frightened and in a lot of pain, wants relief today), and post-whiplash.

Five-second need: *this is common, it's treatable, and it might explain my headaches too.*

### 5. Why necks get sore
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Educate without lecturing, and de-escalate the posture guilt this reader carries. Most have been told their posture is wrong and feel vaguely blamed for it.
**Key points:**
- Sustained positions are the common thread — not because the position is wrong, but because holding any position long enough loads tissue that isn't built to be static
- **Reframe posture honestly:** there's no single correct posture, and "bad posture" is a much weaker predictor of neck pain than most people believe. The better framing is that the best position is the next one — variation matters more than alignment.
- Stress and jaw clenching as genuine load contributors — this is where the TMJ link enters naturally
- Sleep position and pillow height as real, practical factors
- Neck joints, discs and muscles all sensitise; scan findings again often don't match symptoms
- Why the neck refers pain: into the head, the shoulder blade, and down the arm

### 6. Neck pain, headaches and jaw pain
**Component:** standard content + `related-links`
**Direction:** **The page's strategic section and the sibling cross-link requirement (neck ↔ headaches ↔ TMJ).** Explain the connection properly, then hand off. This is where the mega-menu grouping earns its keep.
**Key points:**
- Cervicogenic headache explained simply: the upper neck joints can refer pain into the head, typically starting at the base of the skull and coming over one side. Many people who think they have tension headaches or migraines have a neck contribution.
- The jaw connection: the jaw and upper neck share muscular and neural relationships, and people who clench often have both
- Say plainly that treating these in isolation is why people get partial results

### 7. How we treat it
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** The Alpha process applied to a neck.
**Key points:**
- Assess the neck, the upper back, the shoulders and the jaw where relevant — and ask about the week, not just the pain
- Hands-on treatment: soft tissue, joint mobilisation, dry needling where indicated, manipulation only where appropriate and consented
- Restore mid-back movement, because a stiff thoracic spine leaves the neck doing everything
- Neck and shoulder-blade strength work — the phase most people never get to, and the reason it keeps returning
- Practical changes: desk setup, screen height, driving position, pillow, and how often you move rather than how you sit
- Re-test at the end of every session
