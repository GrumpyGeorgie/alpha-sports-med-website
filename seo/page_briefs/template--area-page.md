> **This is a template brief, not a page brief.** It defines one Astro template plus one content collection with 26 entries. It is written for the developer building the template *and* the writer filling the collection — both need it.

> 🔒 **SLUGS ARE FROZEN.** All 26 URLs exist live, carry equity, and take **no redirect**. `/locations/{suburb}/` stays exactly as-is for every entry, including the inconsistent ones. Do not tidy, do not restructure, do not move them under a `/service-areas/` parent. The only thing changing is what's on the page.

## Purpose

Migrate 26 existing proximity pages into the Astro build without losing their equity, and lift them from their current inconsistent, near-duplicate state to a floor where each entry is individually defensible.

## Reader

Someone in a suburb where Alpha has no premises, searching a modality plus their own suburb name. They are in one of two states:

- **"Is there one near me?"** — they don't know Alpha and are scanning for proximity. They need to learn within five seconds that the nearest clinic is in the next suburb and roughly how far that is. If they have to work it out, they bounce.
- **"How far is it really?"** — they've heard of Alpha or seen the clinic pages and are checking whether the drive is tolerable.

Both need the same first screen: your suburb, the nearest clinic, the drive time, and a book button. Everything else is secondary.

The honest position these pages must take: **Alpha is not in your suburb, but it's close, and here's exactly how close.** Pages that obscure this to seem more local convert worse and read as deceptive. "We're eight minutes up Millers Road in Newport" beats "serving Altona North" every time.

## Search Intent

Commercial with a proximity qualifier. Two structural realities to design around:

1. **These SERPs are local-pack heavy and Alpha is not in the pack** for suburbs where it has no premises — GBP listings show for the clinic's actual location. So area pages compete purely for below-pack organic, plus long-tail suburb+modality strings, plus (increasingly) the "near me" cluster where proximity content contributes relevance signals. Expectations should be set accordingly: these are low-volume, high-count, aggregate-value pages. No single one justifies its build cost; 26 of them with existing equity do.
2. **They already rank for something.** They exist live and have accumulated equity. That makes the migration risk asymmetric — the downside of degrading them exceeds the upside of improving them. Harvest every existing entry before rewriting, and check what each is ranking for. **GSC would make this trivial; access is currently 403-blocked and this is the largest single consequence of that gap in the whole build** — 26 pages migrating semi-blind.

## Architecture

**One Astro template** (`src/pages/locations/[suburb].astro` or equivalent, taking care not to collide with the three clinic pages at the same path depth) **plus one content collection** (`src/content/areas/`) with 26 entries.

⚠️ **Routing note for the developer:** the 26 area pages and the 3 clinic pages sit at the same URL depth (`/locations/{slug}/`). The clinic pages are richer, schema-bearing pages and must not be generated by the area template. Either (a) route clinics explicitly and let the area template handle the rest via a collection lookup, or (b) add a `type` discriminator. Get this decided before either template is built — it is the one genuine technical trap in this migration.

### Shared across all 26 (template-level, not per entry)

Approach/how-we-work block · service list with links · condition cards · practitioner cards pulled from `nearest_clinic` · NAP block pulled from `nearest_clinic` · booking CTA · FAQ shell (with 1 varying question) · breadcrumbs · schema shell.

**The ratio that matters:** an entry that is 90% shared boilerplate with a suburb name substituted is doorway content, and 26 of them is a pattern Google detects easily. The per-entry fields above must produce genuinely different reading experiences, not different noun-slots in identical sentences.

## Thin-Content Floor (non-negotiable per entry)

Every entry must clear all of the following before it ships. An entry that cannot is better left at its current live state than republished thin.

1. **≥450 words total, of which ≥180 are unique to that suburb.** Boilerplate does not count toward the unique figure.
2. **The `local_context` paragraph (80–120 words), written individually.** Not generated, not templated. It should say something true about that suburb's relationship to the clinic — who comes in from there, what they come in with, what's nearby. *"We see a lot of Williamstown Football Club players — a fair few of them are through the door by round six."* That sentence cannot be written by substitution and that is the entire point.
3. **A named route.** The actual road, with a real drive time.
4. **At least two named local landmarks.**
5. **At least one named local club, gym or community connection** — or, where none can be verified, a specific piece of local geography instead. **Never a fabricated relationship.** A resident will notice, and inventing community ties in healthcare advertising is both an AHPRA exposure and a brand-integrity failure against the playbook's community pillar.
6. **One suburb-specific FAQ question** among the four (typically "How long does it take to get to [clinic] from [suburb]?" or a parking/transport question).
7. **No duplicated H2 phrasing across entries** beyond the deliberately shared structural headings.

A useful acceptance test for the writer: **read any two entries back to back. If the only differences are proper nouns, both fail.**

## Sections (repeating, all 26)

1. **Hero** — `hero-banner` (compact). H1 by formula. Immediate proximity statement: suburb → nearest clinic → drive time. Book / call CTAs.
2. **Getting to us from {suburb}** — `nap-block` variant. `route_description`, `drive_time`, `distance_km`, `landmarks`, `transport_note`, `parking_note`, full NAP of the nearest clinic. **This is the page's reason to exist and it goes second, not last.** Most competitor proximity pages bury this.
3. **{Suburb} locals at Alpha** — the `local_context` paragraph plus `local_clubs`. The unique-content engine.
4. **How we work** — `approach-steps`, 3 steps. Shared boilerplate. Problem-led per the playbook: root cause, tailored plan, treatment integrated with rehab. Keep it short — this is the compressible part.
5. **What we treat** — `condition-cards`, 4. Shared, but the *selection* may vary by nearest clinic (running-weighted for Ascot Vale, sports/gym for Newport, back/neck for Bacchus Marsh). Links to condition pages.
6. **Services at {nearest clinic}** — `service-cards`. **Inherits the parent clinic's service box linking rule exactly** — under the osteo-led architecture an Altona North entry's Osteopathy box goes to the main clinic page `/locations/newport/`, its Physio box to `/locations/newport/physiotherapy/`, its Chiro box to `/locations/newport/chiropractic/`, and everything else falls through to the Melbourne `/services/…` page, per the Newport rule. Do not re-derive this per entry; wire it once off `nearest_clinic` so it can never drift.
7. **Who you'd see** — `practitioner-cards`, 2–3 pulled from the nearest clinic's staff. Named and profile-linked. Even on programmatic pages this is E-E-A-T weight and it costs nothing once the CPT exists.
8. **FAQ** — `faq-accordion`, 4. Three shared, one suburb-specific.
9. **Booking** — `cta-section`, single clinic (the nearest one). Not the 3-location block — the page's whole premise is that one clinic is nearest.

## Title Tag Normalisation

Live titles follow an inconsistent `"[Modifier] Osteopathy & Physiotherapy Clinic in/near/serving [Suburb]"` pattern — variable modifiers, three different prepositions, and a modality pair that doesn't match every clinic's actual offering.

**Normalised formula:**

`{Lead Modality} & {Second Modality} {Suburb} | Alpha Sports Medicine`

Applied per nearest clinic:
- **Ascot Vale group (8):** `Osteopath & Physio {Suburb} | Alpha Sports Medicine`
- **Newport group (12):** `Physio & Osteopath {Suburb} | Alpha Sports Medicine`
- **Bacchus Marsh group (6):** `Osteopath {Suburb} | Alpha Sports Medicine` — *pending the Bacchus Marsh scope resolution; see Open Questions*

Rules: drop the "in/near/serving" preposition entirely (it added length, varied inconsistently, and carried no value); no modifiers ("Leading", "Trusted", "Local" — the last is also an AHPRA comparative risk); ≤60 characters, which the longest case (`Physio & Osteopath Williamstown North | Alpha Sports Medicine`, 60) just clears — **check each of the 26 individually and shorten the brand suffix to `| Alpha` on any overflow rather than truncating the suburb.**

**H1 formula:** `{Lead Modality} & {Second Modality} in {Suburb}` — the preposition returns in the H1, where it reads naturally.

**Meta description formula:** `Alpha Sports Medicine is {drive_time} from {Suburb}, in {Nearest Clinic}. {Lead modality}, {second} and rehab that finds the root cause. Book online.` — the fixed portion has been shortened so the longest entry still clears 155. **Worst case assumed: suburb `Williamstown North` (18 chars), drive_time `25 minutes`, clinic `Bacchus Marsh`, modalities `Physiotherapy` + `Osteopathy` = 154.** Still verify ≤155 per entry once the real drive times are in.

**Slugs remain untouched.** Titles change, URLs do not.

## SEO Requirements

- **Internal links in:** `/locations/` hub (grouped by nearest clinic), footer, the nearest clinic's page (a "suburbs we see people from" block), sibling area pages in the same group (2–3 nearest neighbours only — not all 25, which reads as a link farm)
- **Internal links out:** nearest clinic page (prominent, repeated), that clinic's svcloc pages per the linking rule, Melbourne service pages, 4 condition pages, 2–3 staff profiles, Cliniko
- **Schema:** `MedicalWebPage` (not `LocalBusiness` — **Alpha has no premises in these suburbs and asserting a LocalBusiness node for an address it does not occupy is a real spam signal**). Reference the nearest clinic's `LocalBusiness` by `@id` with `areaServed` set to the suburb + postcode. Plus `BreadcrumbList` and `FAQPage`. This is the most commonly botched thing on programmatic proximity pages and getting it right is a genuine differentiator.
- **Breadcrumbs:** Home > Our Clinics > {Suburb}
- **Canonical:** self-referencing. No canonicalising area pages to the clinic page — that would discard the equity this migration exists to preserve.
- **Sitemap:** all 26 in the XML sitemap, standard priority. No noindex.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- **No implied premises.** Nothing may suggest Alpha has a clinic in the suburb. "Alpha Sports Medicine {Suburb}" as a standalone heading implies a location and must not be used — hence the title formula's modality-first construction.
- **No "local" or "leading" modifiers** — the first is misleading for a suburb with no premises, the second is comparative.
- Standard guardrails apply to the shared blocks: no outcome guarantees, no cure language, no comparative claims. Because the blocks are shared, a single non-compliant sentence in boilerplate replicates across 26 pages — **the shared copy needs a compliance review at a higher standard than a one-off page.**
- Practitioner credentials in the shared practitioner cards pull from the CPT; the CPT is the compliance surface (see the staff template brief).

## Voice Notes

Practical and local. These are the least brand-forward pages in the build and should be. The voice comes through in the `local_context` paragraph — that is where a human should be audibly present. Everything else can be efficient.

The single most important stylistic instruction: **write the 26 local_context paragraphs in one sitting, consecutively, by one person.** Written individually across weeks they converge on a template; written together, the writer can see the repetition and push against it.

And still no "performance" — founder-flagged. Use "getting back to what you love".
