## Purpose

Own the exercise physiology term for Melbourne and make the case that this — the loaded, progressive rehab phase — is the part of recovery most people never get, and the reason their injury keeps returning.

## Reader

Split. (1) The **injured active person** who has finished the painful phase, been discharged elsewhere, and re-injured themselves within a couple of months. They may not know the term "exercise physiology"; they know they need someone to tell them what to lift and how much. (2) The **chronic condition** patient — often GP-referred under a care plan for diabetes, cardiovascular disease, osteoarthritis or persistent pain — who has been told to exercise but is frightened of doing it wrong. (3) The **researcher** trying to work out what an EP is and how they differ from a physio.

Five-second need: this is the person who builds the actual programme, they're university-qualified, and rebates may apply.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- Exercise physiologists are **not** AHPRA-registered — they are ESSA-accredited. Do not describe them as AHPRA-registered anywhere. This is a factual accuracy issue that would undermine the whole trust architecture.
- Medicare CDM claims must state the conditions: GP referral required, limited sessions per calendar year, gap fees usually apply. Implying free or unlimited access is misleading.
- No claims that exercise physiology prevents re-injury or cures chronic conditions. "May help", "many patients tell us", "designed to build capacity".
- No comparative claims against physiotherapy or personal training. The PT comparison in the FAQ must describe scope and qualification differences factually, without disparagement.
- Chronic disease content must not stray into implying management of a medical condition outside scope — frame as exercise prescription alongside medical care.
