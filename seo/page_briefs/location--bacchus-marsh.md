## Purpose

Own the Bacchus Marsh location terms below the local pack and serve a regional catchment that behaves differently from the two metro clinics — fewer alternatives, longer drive times, higher intent per search.

## Reader

Someone in Bacchus Marsh, Darley or Maddingley with back or neck pain, or a sports injury from a local club. This catchment differs from Ascot Vale and Newport in ways that should change the copy:

- **Fewer options.** The GBP competitor analysis found five meaningful local competitors, not the 203 seen on the Ascot Vale grid. The reader is not comparing fifteen clinics; they're comparing three or four.
- **Distance is normal.** People here drive to appointments and think in ten-minute radii, not walking distance. Drive-time framing beats "just around the corner".
- **Less metro-inflected.** The playbook's runner/CrossFit/inner-west persona is thinner here. Bacchus Marsh skews to local sporting clubs, tradespeople, families and farming-adjacent work. Copy that assumes a boutique-gym reader will feel imported. Do not simply restyle the Newport copy for a regional town.

## Search Intent

Commercial, suburb-qualified, **local-pack heavy**. Same structural caveat as the other two clinic pages: the page competes for below-pack organic and its deeper job is feeding GBP topical relevance. The upside here is that the competitive field is genuinely thin — a page with real depth, named practitioners and honest local detail can plausibly own the below-pack organic block for the whole Bacchus Marsh cluster (chiropractor 320, physio 260, pilates 140, osteopath 110). That is a realistic goal here in a way it is not in Ascot Vale.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

Small-market pages carry a specific comparative risk: with only four or five local competitors it is tempting to differentiate by implication ("the only clinic in Bacchus Marsh that..."). Even where factually true, exclusivity claims about healthcare provision are hard to defend and are read as comparative advertising. Frame the hypermobility/EDS capability as a stated special interest of the practitioners, not as a market gap. Dr O'Farrell's strength & conditioning qualification must be described accurately — a coaching credential is not an AHPRA registration and the two must not be blurred in the same credential line.
