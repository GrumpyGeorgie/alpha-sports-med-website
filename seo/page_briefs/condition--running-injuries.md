## Purpose

Convert runners searching for someone who actually understands running, and act as the commercial anchor for the entire running cluster — Running Assessment, RunWest, and the running blog posts all point here.

## Reader

A runner with a nagging injury, mid-training-block, with an event in the diary. They have already Googled their symptoms, self-diagnosed (probably wrongly), tried a foam roller and new shoes, and been told by someone to stop running for six weeks. That last instruction is the emotional centre of this page. They are not asking "can I get treatment" — they are asking "will this person make me stop".

Secondary reader: the new or returning runner whose body isn't coping with the load increase, who suspects they're doing something wrong.

Five-second need: *these people run, they get it, and stopping isn't automatically the plan.*

## Search Intent

Commercial. `running physio melbourne` (140) is a hire query and `running injuries` (170) sits just above it as a mixed research term. The volume is modest and that's fine — this page is strategic, not a traffic play. It exists because Alpha's genuine differentiator is running (RunWest, the Running Assessment, the founders' own clinical interest) and because the running blog cluster needs a commercial destination to funnel into.

Do not chase the individual injury head terms here. Shin splints (5,400) and IT band (1,300) belong to their blog posts, which link up to this page.

### 3. "Just stop running for six weeks"
**Component:** `problem-statement`
**Direction:** The page's argument section, and the one the copywriter should spend the most care on. Take the common advice head-on — respectfully, never trash-talking whoever gave it. The playbook is explicit: rest has its place but is rarely the whole answer.
**Key points:**
- Why complete rest often fails: pain settles, tissue capacity drops, you return to the same load that caused it, it comes back in three weeks
- Relative rest and load management explained in plain language — what you can still do, modified, while it settles
- Be honest: some injuries genuinely do need time off running (bone stress being the clear example). Say so. Credibility here is worth more than reassurance.
- Turn: the useful question isn't "how long do I rest" but "why did this tissue stop coping"

### 5. Why running injuries happen
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** The education block. Make the load-versus-capacity argument once, properly, because it's the intellectual foundation of everything Alpha says about recurrence. Salvage whatever holds up from the live page.
**Key points:**
- Load exceeded capacity — either load went up too fast, or capacity dropped (sleep, stress, life, a previous injury), or both
- Training error is the most common contributor: volume jumps, a new surface, a block of speed work added on top of unchanged volume
- Strength and running mechanics as capacity factors — framed as contributors, not causes, and without overclaiming what gait retraining can do
- Footwear addressed honestly: shoes matter less than the internet thinks, and there is no single correct shoe
- Keep the evidence framing cautious. Describe mechanisms as contributors, not certainties.

### 6. How we treat it
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** The Alpha process applied to a runner specifically. This is where the training-load analysis story from the playbook belongs.
**Key points:**
- We ask about your training week before we assess anything — volume, surfaces, sessions, what changed in the four weeks before it started

- Hands-on treatment to settle symptoms, so you can load
- A loading plan: what you run this week, what you don't, and what strength work sits alongside it
- Return-to-running progression with actual criteria rather than a date
- We stay through the strength phase — this is where recurrence gets addressed

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- **Do not promise a race.** "We'll get you to your marathon" is an outcome guarantee. Frame as "we'll build a plan around the date and be honest with you about what's realistic".
- No claims that gait retraining or orthotics prevent injury.
- Bone stress injury guidance must be conservative: if pain is focal, worse with impact, and persists at rest, advise assessment and imaging referral rather than self-managed loading. This is the one genuine red flag on this page and it must be present.
- Do not disparage the practitioner who said "stop running". Frame as incomplete advice, not wrong advice.
