## Purpose

**The single most important new page in the rebuild.** Knee Pain is the only major page with no existing equity, sitting under a 12,100/mo head term with 2,400/mo of direct treatment intent. It has to be built to compete from zero — which means it has to be the best page on the topic Alpha can produce, not a template fill.

Its job: capture the largest untapped condition demand on the site and convert four distinct knee populations without becoming a directory of knee diagnoses.

## Reader

Knee pain has the widest demographic spread of any condition Alpha treats, and the page must serve four readers without diluting. Write for all four, in this order of commercial value:

1. **The runner / active adult, 25–45.** Pain at the front of the knee, or on the outside. Worse going downhill and down stairs, worse after sitting (the "cinema sign"), worse as mileage climbs. Has been told they have runner's knee, or that their glutes are weak, or that they need orthotics. Has probably tried a strap and some clamshells. **Highest volume, highest intent, best fit for Alpha's brand.**
2. **The gym-goer.** Pain with squatting, lunging or leg day. Terrified of "wearing out" the knee. Often has been told to stop squatting below parallel.
3. **The 50+ knee.** Aching, stiff in the morning, worse after standing or walking. Has had an X-ray showing osteoarthritis and has been told it's wear and tear and they'll need a replacement eventually. **This reader has been told the most damaging thing and is the one the page can help most.** They are also less likely to see themselves in a sports clinic — the page has to actively include them.
4. **The acute knee.** Twisted it, swollen, unstable, or locking. Needs assessment and possibly imaging. Smallest group, highest urgency.

Common thread across all four: they've been given a mechanical explanation ("wear and tear", "your knees are shot", "bad tracking") that makes the knee sound broken, and they've reduced what they do because of it.

Five-second need: *knee pain is usually manageable, and stopping isn't the plan.*

## Search Intent

Commercial, high volume, high competition (`knee pain treatment` 2,400, comp 1.00 — the most competitive term in the entire keyword map). Nationally saturated by hospital groups, health portals and large clinic chains.

**Strategic implication:** Alpha will not outrank healthdirect or a hospital network for the national head term, and should not be briefed as though it will. This page competes on three fronts instead:

1. **Local pack and local organic** — "knee pain treatment melbourne", "knee physio near me". This is where the wins are and it's why the location block and NAP consistency matter more here than anywhere.
2. **Long-tail symptom queries** — "knee pain going down stairs", "knee pain when squatting", "front of knee pain running", "knee pain after sitting". These are lower competition, high intent, and the section structure below is deliberately designed around them. **Each symptom sub-section is a long-tail ranking target and should carry the query language naturally in its heading or first sentence.**
3. **AI extraction and featured snippets** — the FAQ block and the symptom sections are written for direct answer extraction.

Judge this page's early performance on long-tail impressions and local pack presence, not on the head term. Set that expectation with the client at sign-off.

### 4. Where does it hurt?
**Component:** interactive or visual `condition-cards` block (design system: consider a simple knee diagram with clickable regions, falling back to cards)
**Direction:** **The page's structural centrepiece and its main long-tail engine.** Knee pain is unusually well differentiated by location, which makes this both clinically sound and an SEO asset. Four to five regions, each 100–150 words, each written to answer a real search query. This section alone is roughly 500 words and it earns them.
**Key points:**
- **Front of the knee / around the kneecap** — patellofemoral pain, "runner's knee". Worse down stairs and hills, worse after sitting, worse with squats. The most common presentation by a distance. Address the "maltracking" and "bad kneecap alignment" explanation directly: it's an outdated framing and it makes people afraid of their own knee. What's usually going on is load exceeding what the knee currently tolerates.
- **Below the kneecap** — patellar tendinopathy (jumper's knee). Point tender on the tendon, worse with jumping and landing, worse the morning after. Link → /conditions/sports-injuries/tendonitis-tendinopathy/
- **Outside of the knee** — IT band-related pain. Common in runners and cyclists, often at a consistent distance into a run. **Two sentences and a link only — the IT band blog post owns this (see guard).**
- **Inside of the knee** — medial joint line pain, meniscal irritation, MCL, and the most common location for osteoarthritis-related pain
- **Behind the knee** — tightness, swelling, a Baker's cyst. Brief.
- Each region should name the everyday activity that provokes it, because that's how people search: stairs, squatting, kneeling, driving, sitting, getting out of the car.

### 5. "It's just wear and tear"
**Component:** `problem-statement`
**Direction:** **The page's most important argument and the section that will earn its links and its shares.** Reader 3 has been told their knee is worn out. That framing is both clinically outdated and behaviourally destructive — it makes people stop moving, which makes knees worse. Reframe it properly, at length, and without dismissing anyone's pain or their X-ray.
**Key points:**
- Osteoarthritis is extremely common on imaging in people with no pain at all, and the degree of change on an X-ray correlates poorly with how much pain someone has. Say it plainly.
- "Wear and tear" implies a tyre wearing out — a finite amount of use and then failure. Cartilage isn't like that: joints are living tissue that responds to load. Give the reader a better metaphor and they'll behave differently.
- Movement and strengthening are the recommended first-line management for knee osteoarthritis in essentially every clinical guideline. This is not a fringe position and it's worth saying so.
- The essential qualifier: this does not mean the pain isn't real, and it doesn't mean surgery is never appropriate
- What the reader is likely to conclude if they don't read this: stop walking, stop the gym, wait for a replacement. That path leads to a weaker knee, a heavier body and more pain — and it is worth naming that trajectory gently and directly.
- 300–400 words. Do not compress this section.

### 6. Runners and gym-goers: the load conversation
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Readers 1 and 2. This is where Alpha's actual expertise shows and where the page differentiates from every generic knee page.
**Key points:**
- The common pattern: pain follows a change — mileage, hills, a new program, a return after a break, more intensity on the same volume
- Why "your glutes are weak" is an incomplete answer: it's a plausible contributor, but it's become a catch-all diagnosis and it doesn't explain why it started when it did
- **Squats and knees:** knees travelling over toes is not dangerous, and deep squatting does not wear knees out. Common bad advice, addressed head-on. Loading the knee through range is generally how it gets more tolerant.
- Running mechanics and cadence as contributors, framed cautiously — link → /running-assessment/
- Orthotics addressed honestly: sometimes helpful for some people, not a universal answer, and not a substitute for capacity
- Modify, don't stop: reduce hills, adjust range, change tempo, drop volume temporarily while strength builds

### 7. How we treat knee pain
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** The Alpha process, applied specifically. Six steps.
**Key points:**
- History first — what you do, what changed, what you've been told, what you've stopped doing
- Assess the knee, and the hip, ankle and foot, because the knee sits between two joints that dictate what it has to absorb
- Explain what we think is going on in words you can repeat to someone else
- Hands-on treatment to settle symptoms enough to load
- **Progressive strengthening — the primary treatment for most knee pain, not an add-on.** Say this explicitly. Quads, hips and calves, loaded properly and progressed over months.
- Return to the actual thing: running, squatting, stairs, hiking, the golf course, keeping up with grandkids
- Re-test at the end of every session
- Note what's on site for the strength phase — exercise physiology, clinical pilates, S&C — because this is where most people get discharged and stall

### 8. When surgery is part of the conversation
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Short, balanced, credibility-building. Include it precisely because it makes the conservative argument trustworthy rather than self-serving.
**Key points:**
- Knee replacement is an effective operation for advanced osteoarthritis that hasn't responded to conservative management — say so plainly
- The order matters: guidelines recommend exercise, load management and weight management first, and many people improve enough that surgery moves off the table or further away
- Arthroscopy for degenerative meniscal tears and osteoarthritis has largely fallen out of favour — mention neutrally and without campaigning
- Post-surgical rehab is something Alpha does, and it's worth saying
- Every surgical decision belongs to the patient and their surgeon

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- **The osteoarthritis reframe must be handled carefully.** It is evidence-based and appropriate, but it must never read as "your arthritis isn't real" or "you don't need surgery". Always attach the qualifier that pain is real and that surgical decisions belong with the patient and their surgeon.
- No claim that exercise reverses osteoarthritis or regenerates cartilage. Exercise is described as recommended first-line management for symptoms and function — that's accurate and sufficient.
- The arthroscopy point must be neutral and non-campaigning. Do not tell people not to have a procedure.
- No claim that treatment prevents knee replacement.
- Squat and loading advice must be general and not read as an exercise prescription for an unassessed reader.
- Red flags block is mandatory — the hot, swollen, unwell knee (septic arthritis) is the one that genuinely matters.
- Timeframes as ranges with caveats. Knee rehab is measured in months.

## Voice Notes

- **This page has to hold two tones at once.** The runner needs peer-level confidence; the 60-year-old needs warmth and inclusion without being patronised. The bridge is that both have been told to be careful with their knee and both deserve better than that.
- **Do not let the page read as young and athletic only.** Language, examples and imagery must include walking, stairs, gardening, standing at work and getting out of a car alongside running and squatting. Reader 3 is a large share of the volume and will bounce from a page that isn't for them.
- The "wear and tear" reframe is the emotional centre. Write it as liberation, delivered gently — this person has been carrying a sentence for years.
- Be specific about activities throughout. "Knee pain" is abstract; "the knee that complains on the way down the stairs at the station" is not.
- No "performance". No "bone on bone". No "your knees are shot" even in quotation. No "wear and tear" used without immediately unpacking it.
