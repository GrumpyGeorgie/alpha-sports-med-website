## Purpose

Take the treatment intent under a 10,000/mo head term, and reframe back pain for a reader who has been frightened by a scan report or a diagnosis and is now moving less because of it.

## Reader

The widest audience of any page on the site, but with a consistent emotional profile. Three arrivals:

1. **Acute.** Went two days ago — bent to pick something up, or woke with it. Can barely move. Frightened it's a disc.
2. **Recurrent.** Goes every few months. Has a routine of managing it. Has never been told why it happens.
3. **Chronic.** Months or years. Has had scans, has been given a diagnosis with alarming words in it (degeneration, bulging disc, arthritis), has been told to be careful with their back, and has consequently stopped doing things. This reader is the one Alpha can help most and the one the page should be written for.

Five-second need: *this is usually less sinister than it feels, and someone will actually explain it.*

### 2. Red flags
**Component:** alert / callout block
**Direction:** Compact, above the fold, unfussy. Back pain has a small set of genuine emergencies and they must be stated.
**Key points:**
- **Seek urgent medical care (emergency department) if:** loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the saddle/groin area, progressive weakness in both legs, or back pain after significant trauma
- **See your GP promptly if:** back pain with unexplained weight loss, fever, night pain that wakes you and doesn't ease with position change, a history of cancer, or if you're on long-term steroids or have osteoporosis
- Keep it brief and non-alarming in tone. The rest of the page is about de-escalating fear, and this block shouldn't undercut that.

### 4. What's probably going on
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** The page's intellectual centre. Reframe carefully, accurately, and without dismissing pain.
**Key points:**
- The great majority of back pain is non-specific: it comes from the joints, discs, muscles and nerves of the back working together, and no single structure gets identified. That's normal and it's not a failure of diagnosis.
- **Scan findings:** disc bulges, degeneration and arthritis appear commonly on scans of people with no pain at all, and increase with age like grey hair. Say this. It is the single most therapeutically valuable sentence on the page for reader 3, and it is well supported.
- The essential qualifier: this does not mean the pain isn't real or isn't physical. Pain is real; the scan just isn't the whole story.
- Backs are strong and built for load. Bending isn't dangerous. Protecting a back indefinitely tends to make it worse, not safer.
- Why it hurts so much if there's no damage — sensitivity, muscle guarding, fear of movement, and load exceeding what the tissue currently tolerates
- 300–400 words. Take the space.

### 5. Sciatica and leg pain
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Necessary sub-topic — it's the presentation people are most frightened by and it has its own search demand.
**Key points:**
- What sciatica actually means: nerve-related pain travelling into the buttock and leg, often with pins and needles or numbness
- Common causes, described without over-attributing to discs
- The reassuring and accurate point: most nerve-related leg pain settles over weeks to months, and surgery is the exception rather than the rule
- When it needs review: progressive weakness, worsening numbness, or the red flags above
- Note there is a sciatica blog draft in progress — this section stays a summary and links out

### 6. Why it keeps coming back
**Component:** `problem-statement`
**Direction:** Readers 2 and 3's section and the commercial core. The playbook's recurrence pillar in its purest form.
**Key points:**
- The typical cycle: it goes, you rest, it settles, you resume exactly what you were doing, it goes again in four months
- What's usually missing: nobody worked out what the back was being asked to do and why it stopped coping
- Contributors, named specifically: a big sedentary load, a job with repeated bending or lifting, a training program with no back-strength work, sleep, stress, a period off training, a new baby, a house move
- Fear-avoidance named and explained gently — moving less and bracing more makes backs more sensitive, not less
- The turn: the goal isn't a back that never hurts, it's a back that tolerates your life

### 7. How we treat back pain
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** The Alpha process. Emphasise the two things that distinguish it — the explanation, and staying through the strength phase.
**Key points:**
- History first: what you do, what changed, what you've been told, what you've stopped doing because of it
- Assess the back, the hips and the way you move and load — not just the sore segment
- Explain what we think is going on in language you can repeat to someone else. Most patients have never had this.
- Hands-on treatment to reduce pain and guarding so you can move
- Graded return to movement and loading, built to your actual life
- Strength work — this is where recurrence is addressed, and it's the phase most people never get to
- Re-test at the end of every session

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- **The scan-findings reframe is evidence-based and appropriate, but it must never read as "your scan doesn't matter" or discourage someone from following medical advice.** Frame as context, always with the "your pain is real" qualifier attached.
- Red flags block is mandatory. Cauda equina symptoms must be listed explicitly.
- No claim that treatment cures back pain or prevents recurrence. "Aims to reduce the chance of it recurring" is the ceiling.
- Manipulation described as consent-led and optional. No claims about realigning anything.
- Section 8 must not be comparative. "We won't tell you your back is fragile" is a statement of Alpha's approach; "unlike other clinics" is a breach.
- Sciatica surgery framing must not steer anyone away from a surgical opinion.

## Voice Notes

- Reassuring and de-escalating throughout. The dominant job is lowering fear that other information sources raised.

- The scan reframe should feel like relief, not a correction. Nobody has told them this before and it will land hard if delivered warmly.
- Confident on movement. This is a page that should make someone feel safer bending over.
- No "performance". No "realign". No "slipped disc".
