## Purpose

Consolidate Alpha's existing TMJ blog equity into a proper condition page, and capture a small, low-competition local term with an unusually high conversion rate because so few clinics say they treat jaws.

## Reader

Usually knows exactly what they've got. Has often already seen a dentist, may have been given a night splint, and may have been told there isn't much else to do. Frequently a clencher or grinder, frequently under sustained stress, and frequently also has neck pain and headaches without having connected them.

Presentations: jaw pain with chewing, clicking or popping, jaw locking or catching, morning jaw ache and tightness, facial pain, ear-area pain with normal ear examinations, headaches at the temples.

Emotional state: mildly embarrassed and quite frustrated. It's an odd thing to have and people don't take it seriously. Many have been told to "try to relax".

Five-second need: *this clinic actually treats jaws.* Say it early and plainly.

## Search Intent

Commercial and explicitly local — `tmj treatment melbourne` (110, comp 0.17). Low volume, low competition, high intent. Nobody searching this is browsing.

The bigger strategic value is that TMJ is a genuine differentiator: most physios and chiros don't advertise jaw treatment. Being visible for it captures a patient who has been looking for someone specifically.

### 3. What the TMJ is and what goes wrong
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Brief and functional. **Salvage the live `/tmj-and-jaw-pain/` blog post here** — it's the primary source and its explanatory content should form this section.
**Key points:**
- The temporomandibular joint: a small, hard-working joint that both hinges and slides, with a disc inside it, used thousands of times a day
- Common presentations: muscular (the chewing muscles overworking, usually from clenching), joint (disc-related clicking, catching or locking), or both
- **Clicking without pain is very common and usually not a problem in itself.** Say this early — it's reassuring, accurate, and pre-empts unnecessary worry.
- What tends to matter more: pain, limited opening, locking, or a change in the pattern
- Why it refers to the ear, temple and side of the head — explains a symptom people find alarming

### 4. Why it happens
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Short. Focus on load, because that's what treatment addresses.
**Key points:**
- Clenching and grinding, often at night and often unnoticed — stress-linked, and named without judgement
- Sustained load: chewing gum, nail biting, hard foods, long dental procedures
- Neck contribution — the upper neck and jaw share muscular and neural relationships, and a stiff, loaded neck often accompanies a sore jaw
- Habit and posture-of-the-jaw factors: resting teeth together (they shouldn't touch at rest), holding the tongue and jaw braced
- Occasional other causes worth a mention: arthritis, trauma, hypermobility → /conditions/hypermobility/ (genuine and often-missed connection)

### 5. How we treat it
**Component:** `approach-steps`
**Direction:** Demonstrate that this is a real, specific skill and not a generic massage.
**Key points:**
- Assess jaw movement, the chewing muscles, the joint itself, and the neck
- Hands-on treatment of the jaw muscles — note that some of this is done intra-orally, with gloves and consent, and explain it plainly so nobody is surprised in the room
- Treat the neck and upper back as part of the same picture
- Jaw exercises, resting position retraining, and habit awareness — the part patients do between sessions and the part that changes it
- Practical load management: soft-food periods when it's irritable, gum, chewing habits, sleep position
- Work alongside your dentist — splint therapy is theirs, and we say so. Refer back where a dental cause is likely.

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

- No claim that treatment cures TMJ disorder or stops grinding.
- **Intra-oral technique must be described as consent-led, gloved, optional, and explained beforehand.** Given the sensitivity of intra-oral treatment, the page should make it clear a patient can decline and still be treated. Non-negotiable, and worth a compliance read.
- Scope boundary with dentistry must be explicit. Do not advise on splints, occlusion, bite adjustment or dental treatment.
- No claim to diagnose TMJ disorders where a dental or medical cause may be present.
- Persistent facial pain has other differentials — include a light "if it isn't responding as expected, it should be reviewed" line.
