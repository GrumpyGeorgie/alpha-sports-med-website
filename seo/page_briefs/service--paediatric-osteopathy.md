## Purpose

Reach parents considering osteopathic treatment for a baby or child, and do it with the honesty and restraint that both AHPRA and a nervous parent require.

## Reader

An exhausted parent, usually of a baby under six months, usually in the first weeks of a problem they didn't expect. They are worried, sleep-deprived, and have already been Googling at 3am — the playbook's "catastrophising" reader in its most acute form. They may have been recommended osteopathy by a maternal health nurse, a midwife, a mothers' group or another parent.

They are also, very often, **sceptical or conflicted**: they've read that paediatric osteopathy is contested, and they're trying to work out whether they're being sensible or desperate.

A smaller group are parents of older children — growing pains, sports injuries in teenagers, postural concerns.

Five-second need: these are registered practitioners, the treatment is gentle, nobody will make claims they can't support, and someone will actually listen to what's been happening.

### 3. What treatment actually involves
**Component:** standard content
**Direction:** Demystify. Parents imagine chiropractic-style manipulation and are frightened of it. Describe what actually happens in plain terms.
**Key points:**
- Thorough history first: pregnancy, birth, feeding, sleep, development
- Gentle techniques only — light contact, soft tissue and gentle movement. No high-velocity manipulation on infants.
- Babies are often awake, held by a parent, and may feed during the session
- The parent stays in the room throughout
- Session length and what to bring
- What the osteopath will and won't do — and that they will refer on if something is outside their scope

### 4. What osteopathy can and can't help with
**Component:** content block
**Direction:** **The most important section on the page, and the one that most competitor pages get catastrophically wrong.** Write it as an act of integrity. This page should be the most conservative claims page on the site and the parent will recognise that restraint as trustworthiness.
**Key points:**
- Osteopathy is musculoskeletal care. It addresses how a baby's body moves and where it is uncomfortable or restricted.
- **Explicitly state what it does not treat:** colic, reflux, ear infections, sleep problems, feeding difficulties as medical conditions, allergies, developmental conditions. There is not good evidence for osteopathic treatment of these, and AHPRA has taken action over such claims.
- Where a musculoskeletal factor may be contributing to a presentation, say so in exactly those terms — contributing, may, assess.
- Position the honesty as the point: "If we don't think we can help, we'll tell you, and we'll help you work out who can."

## AHPRA Compliance Notes

**This is the single highest-compliance-risk page on the site. It should not be published without client and, ideally, professional-indemnity-insurer review.**

- **Prohibited:** any claim, implication or testimonial suggesting osteopathy treats or improves colic, reflux, sleep, feeding, ear infections, allergies, immunity, "birth trauma" as a diagnosis, or developmental conditions. AHPRA and the Osteopathy Board have taken enforcement action over exactly these claims, and there is standing regulatory attention on manual therapy for infants.
- **Testimonials from parents are extremely high-risk** and should be omitted entirely. A parent saying "our baby finally slept" is a prohibited outcome claim regardless of framing.
- No before/after imagery. No imagery implying a therapeutic result (sleeping baby after treatment).
- The "seek medical advice first" statement must be prominent, not buried in a footer.
- No use of "specialist" — "osteopaths with additional training in paediatrics".
- Language throughout: "assess", "may contribute", "many parents tell us", "designed to". Never "treats", "resolves", "settles".
- Have a named clinician sign off the clinical content.
